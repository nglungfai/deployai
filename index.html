    How it works

    Rules see what customers did. A behavioral model sees what they'll do next.

    Most brands score customers with recency and spend rules or the generic scores built into their email tools. A behavioral model reads the whole sequence: what someone browsed, skipped, opened and ordered, week by week.

    FAQ

    Questions data and marketing teams ask us.

    Does our customer data have to leave our environment?

    No. We build and run the model inside your own cloud and warehouse, and scores are written back to the tools you already use. Your customer records stay yours and are used only for your models. We keep only anonymous, combined learnings across clients, like which behaviors tend to come before churn, so every new model starts smarter.

    How much data do we need?

    Roughly 100,000 or more customers with at least a year of order history. Browsing, email and app events make the model stronger but aren't required to start.

    How is this different from the predictive scores in our email or CDP tool?

    Built-in scores are generic models trained on summary fields. We train on the full sequence of your customers' behavior, and we prove the result against a holdout. If your current scores already do as well, the pilot will show it and you'll have spent 6–8 weeks finding out.

    How did you measure the results on this page?

    They come from a synthetic brand of 200,000 customers over three years, built so that the true drivers of churn and of offer response are known. Models trained on earlier weeks were scored on a later win-back campaign that randomly held back 20% of eligible customers. Extra orders are the difference between contacted and held-back customers within each targeted group.

    How long until we see results?

    A pilot takes 6–8 weeks: about 4 to build and validate the model, then a campaign readout once its holdout window closes.
